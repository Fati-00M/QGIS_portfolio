Pakistan Randolph Glacier Inventory

RGI version 7 shapefiles for the South Asia West region, clipped by mask layer to a Pakistan boundary, then classified with graduated symbology into five area classes and composed in the QGIS Print Layout. Paired with a ranked table of the ten largest bodies — Siachen at 1,087.9 km², Baltoro at 790 km², Biafo at 532 km². RGI v7 approximates glacier area to the year 2000, giving a fixed baseline for any glacial lake outburst flood or water-security assessment.

Question: Where are Pakistan's largest mapped glaciers within the RGI baseline?

Method: RGI v7 outlines were clipped to Pakistan, classified by area, and presented with a ranked table in QGIS.

Decision use: The inventory can support screening for glacier research and water-security assessment. It is a fixed circa-2000 baseline, not a current hazard forecast.

Wah Cantt — Critical Services & Accessibility

Every feature on this sheet was digitised by hand over the OpenStreetMap Standard basemap — six point layers for banks, bus stops, hospitals, groceries, educational institutions and mosques, a polyline layer for roads, and polygon layers for parks and tree cover, all threaded onto the GT Road / Jinnah Avenue corridor and composed in the QGIS Print Layout. Each class carries its own symbol and label so the sheet stays readable printed in greyscale: the level of detail a response planner actually needs, and a demonstration of vector creation, styling and labelling end to end.

Featured case study

Pakistan Urban Extent & Exposure

A screening-level view of how Pakistan's settlement pattern changes from 1975 to 2030, and what that evidence can—and cannot—support.

Context

Settlement and population growth shape where people, infrastructure, and services may be exposed to climate hazards. A consistent national time series can help identify places that warrant deeper resilience, infrastructure, and financial assessment.

Question

Where is built-up and population exposure increasing across Pakistan between 1975 and 2030?

Data

Source
European Commission JRC, GHS-SMOD R2023A
Dates
1975–2030 at five-year intervals; 2025 and 2030 are projections
Resolution
1 km source grid for the settlement classification
Coverage
Global source data clipped to Pakistan
Access checked
15 September 2026

Method

  1. Acquire the GHSL settlement-classification layers for each five-year epoch.
  2. Clip the layers to Pakistan and retain the relevant degree-of-urbanisation classes.
  3. Apply consistent categorised symbology in QGIS across all time steps.
  4. Assign a temporal date to each layer and inspect the sequence with the QGIS temporal controller.
  5. Compose the 2030 print layout and export the time series as a web-ready animation.

Decision relevance

This analysis can help identify areas for deeper climate-risk, infrastructure, or service-access assessment. A financing decision would still require hazard data, asset-level exposure, vulnerability assumptions, costs, and financial analysis.

Limitations

  • GHSL is a modelled global dataset and does not replace locally validated census, cadastral, or infrastructure records.
  • The 2025 and 2030 layers are projections and carry model uncertainty; the visual sequence does not establish why change occurred.
  • A 1 km national screening layer cannot support site-level design, valuation, lending, or investment decisions on its own.
  • The published map uses EPSG:4326 for display; area and distance analysis would require an appropriate projected CRS.

Reproducibility

Study period: 1975–2030. Published CRS: EPSG:4326 / WGS 84. Software: QGIS. This urban-extent output uses prepared GHSL layers; it does not claim a Python or Earth Engine processing step that is not documented for this specific product. See the project method notes and the source dataset record.
